# Frontend implementation intent

Visual thesis: an edge-to-edge, dark globe is the workspace. Quiet floating controls, blue-grey geography and a single pale-cyan accent keep attention on the world. No permanent sidebar, dashboard heading or investigation prompt competes with the map.

Map reading (26 October 2026, after a second comparison with Recall): report clusters take the colour of their most common event group and count reports, with white numbers; a region-level report (country, province, sea) is one translucent ring inside its region, coloured the same way, clustered together with point reports. Region outlines appear only while their ring is hovered; a selected message's regions get a thin dashed outline. Feed rows show the source's own words (translated) rather than the model headline, and the place as the most specific site in bold with its country beside it ("Dar'a  Syria", "+1" for more places). The Map menu offers Dark map or Satellite (Esri imagery with OpenFreeMap borders and labels). Clicking a feed row (or a marker) previews the message as a card on the map, as Recall did: the map flies to its point or region, the card shows type, place, the source's words, source and time, and Open shows the full message; the feed stays open. A message with no place opens in full.

Content plan: Map, feed, message. Feed and Sources are primary; More contains only filtering and Processing. The message panel sizes to its content and contains source/time, one readable text body, controlled media, relevant map links and a "Same event" timeline of linked reports (oldest first, the open message marked, a Map action fitting their places). The feed opens on Event reports (All messages one click away). Each row reads as a ticker line: a compact time ("36m", "2h", "10 Oct"; full UTC on hover), a dot in the event's map colour (an empty ring when there is no event), the place in bold, or the event type when no place is reported (then "no place" on the right), the event type on the right, a two-line headline and the source in small print. One palette per event group is shared by map markers and feed dots. A possible event (Jev's suggestion where the main reading found no event) shows a dashed ring in the type's colour and "possible"; the reader shows the type with "possible", and hover text says Jev suggested it. A "N reports" chip marks linked reports (not independent confirmation); same-event reports fold under the newest row as "Same event · N reports". The Layers button in the left map tools opens the live layers panel: one search field finds a ship (name, MMSI or IMO) or a flight (exact callsign, flight number or registration), loaded ones instantly and then from the providers; switches for ships, aircraft and maritime warnings, each with a status line; ship type chips with live counts, an Under way / Stopped filter and report age; aircraft category chips (airliners and cargo, military and government, business jets, light aircraft, helicopters, gliders/balloons/drones, ground vehicles) with All aircraft / Only military and government links and an on-ground toggle; live only, beneath them, any aircraft whose feed status is an emergency, Flightradar24's ten most-tracked flights (rank, name, route, full type, people watching; "in view" when drawn; choosing one selects it and flies there) and the military and government aircraft in view grouped by the state their callsign or Mode S address names, with role counts, each state opening its aircraft. Ships come from KeelWatch vector tiles and are coloured by type (red is military on both layers): coloured dots up to zoom 6.5 (a dot may stand for several ships when zoomed out, drawn slightly larger), then hull shapes rotated by heading and sized by reported length (moving) or round marks (stopped), with names from zoom 10.5. Clicking empty map, the X or Escape ends a selection. Aircraft use silhouettes from the provider's icon class and type code (narrow- and wide-body jets, four-engine, business jet, turboprop, light aircraft, helicopter, fast jet, drone, balloon, ground vehicle), yellow for civil, red for provider-listed military or government, grey for ground vehicles, dimmed on the ground, with callsigns from zoom 7.5 (military ones tinted). Emergency and military aircraft draw above other traffic. Each aircraft trails a short line, fading towards its tail, through the positions Relic received for it in the last three minutes (a gap or impossible jump restarts it); an aircraft whose feed status is an emergency has a slowly pulsing red ring. Hovering previews a ship (name, type, flag, status, speed, destination, report age) or aircraft (a military and government badge, the operator its callsign names or else, for state aircraft, its Mode S state, with the state's code; callsign, flight number and registration; full type name, code and, for military and government aircraft, a role from the type; route; altitude with climb or descent between its last two reports, ground speed and heading; position source and age; an emergency banner when the feed says so); report markers still take hover and click priority. Selecting opens an instrument-style card toned by the ship category or aircraft class: ships show a compass (course arrow, hull at heading), speed, status pill, voyage from last port to destination, a speed profile along the 6 h / 24 h / 3 d / 7 d track, identity codes, a to-scale hull bar, former names and the messages the ship finder links to the ship; aircraft show the provider photo with credit, an emergency banner when the feed says so, a route bar with the aircraft at its progress, altitude with climb rate, ground speed and track, an altitude profile, identity codes, the operator (Flightradar24's airline name, else what the callsign or flight number names), the registration state from the Mode S address, the role of a military or government type, the position source and the most-tracked rank. Operator, state, type-name and role tables are Relic's own and partial: unknown values are left out, never guessed; military status is only ever the provider's category. Paths on the map use the same colour scales as the profiles: ship tracks by reported speed, flight paths by altitude, on a dark casing. Cards can centre or follow the selection. The Layers panel opens with Live / Past moment: a past moment (UTC field in ten-minute steps and hour steps, or sent from the reader) shows ships from KeelWatch's past tiles in amber-marked chrome with a "Ships at …" badge that also names any KeelWatch collection gap around the moment, warnings registered by then and no aircraft; a historical snapshot uses its own time. The ship finder notes when KeelWatch was not collecting for part of a message's window. Feed rows show the ship finder's vessels as small chips (confirmed solid, suggested dashed). Processing has a Ship finder lane: flagged and checked counts, Find ships in the waiting messages, Stop, and an automatic switch for new messages. A message that probably names ships shows a quiet Ships block under its event timeline: "Find ships" sends the text to the model once (the cost is shown after), and each named vessel lists its KeelWatch candidates with type, flag, identifiers, size, last report, whether it was near the message's places around its time, and where the message agrees or differs. Each candidate can be shown on the map (its card and track around the message time), confirmed or rejected; the name opens the exact quote in the original. "Which ships were near the reported places?" lists the vessels KeelWatch recorded there around the message time, and "Every ship at this moment" switches the map to that past moment. Original and Lineage are on demand. Briefs, notebooks, manual assessments and watches are removed from normal navigation. Processing shows one state with Pause/Resume, a reading-coverage bar, the reading lane with Jev and event-link branches, and a merged activity line. A message's Lineage view draws its recorded path (source → collected → read → located → in the feed, with version, Jev and related-message branches) and opens one card per selected step. No other provenance dialog remains. The map status is a plain readout with no coverage dialog. Source and data contracts stay intact.

Interaction thesis: globe navigation, a short drawer entrance, a responsive place-search palette and the lineage line drawing itself when opened are the moments of motion. The globe supports detailed vector geography, globe/flat projection, zoom, compass, fit, clustering and online display geography. Camera and report visibility never silently alter evidence scope. Only explicit filtering changes the shared map/list/count scope. Reduced motion removes animated movement.

This supersedes the initial investigation-first composition at the owner's request on 10 September 2026. Google Earth and the supplied World Monitor/Polyglobe references inform the full-canvas hierarchy, not additional source capabilities. Public OpenFreeMap tiles provide display geography; they are never intelligence or historical evidence. There is no offline basemap mode; an unavailable online map has an explicit retry state. Accepted reference inputs remain retained as provenance, not an automatic fallback.

Native accessible controls implement the initial component behavior; TanStack Query owns backend cache, TanStack Virtual bounds rows, MapLibre owns its mounted map, and React Flow renders actual API lineage. Draft text is local until durable acknowledgement, recovered by notebook/brief ID; assessment drafts additionally bind exact citation, scope and publication. URL state contains stable IDs and the explicit scope, never private configuration.

Owner clarification, 10 September 2026: optimize the coming real-data milestone for immediate situational awareness: open the map → see incoming reports/events → inspect related source posts and media → understand changes. Evaluate simplicity against actual source content. Do not let notebook, brief or technical configuration flows drive the everyday experience. Existing exact evidence, separate judgments, source limitations and proxy contracts remain intact. Grouped events and live collection are pending capabilities, not a relabeling of the current fictional reports.


Surface roles, following the next owner review: Feed/report inspection is a right-side overlay. Sources and Processing use centered, bounded management dialogs. Menus dismiss without rebuilding the map. No fake active state or throughput is shown.

Ships and warnings, 26 October 2026: the Ships section of Layers has a source choice, KeelWatch (collected, with history) or MarineTraffic (live map), each with a health dot; the choice is remembered per browser. When KeelWatch is not configured, unreachable, needs attention or its tiles fail, an amber card offers "Use MarineTraffic". MarineTraffic ships use the same symbols, colours, filters, counts, hover and card language as KeelWatch ships; the card adds the provider photo, the voyage from last to next port with MarineTraffic's progress marker, departure and broadcast ETA, draught, load condition and deadweight, class and identity, and says that there is no track because Relic keeps no MarineTraffic history. Satellite contacts read "Satellite contact, identity withheld". MarineTraffic is live only: at a past moment its layer is empty and the badge and panel say so, with "Use KeelWatch". Ship reports fade with age (full to 30 minutes, about two-fifths by a day), for both sources. Maritime warnings filter by registration time in the last 6 h, 24 h, 3 d, 7 d, 30 d, 90 d or all; the status line gives the count in the window "of N retained", and a newest-first list (four rows, "Show all" for up to 50) shows type, reference, the place and what happened, and age; choosing one opens its card on the map. Attacks, hijacks and kidnaps draw slightly larger, warnings registered within a day carry a soft ring, and the warning tooltip and card take the type colour.
